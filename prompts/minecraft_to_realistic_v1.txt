Task: Reconstruct the provided Minecraft-style scene as a true photorealistic photograph of its real-world counterpart.

Priority 1 - Preserve scene identity and composition:
- Keep the same camera viewpoint, projection, framing, horizon, and crop.
- Keep the same semantic objects and terrain regions, with the same object count, dominant colors, relative positions, depth ordering, broad silhouettes, and scale relationships.
- Keep foreground, middle-ground, background, and empty-space placement consistent with the source.
- Treat the scene inventory as closed: do not introduce new buildings, props, terrain features, people, animals, vegetation masses, weather elements, or background landmarks, and do not remove source elements.

Priority 2 - Fundamentally re-imagine local geometry:
- Treat the source pixels and voxel regions as semantic and compositional evidence, not as literal surface geometry.
- Replace every block staircase, cube cluster, grid edge, and pixelated contour with the continuous natural or manufactured form that the depicted real-world object would have.
- Use smooth terrain, irregular natural erosion, continuous roofs and walls, rounded organic forms, realistic foliage, and clean manufactured joints wherever semantically appropriate.
- Preserving composition does not mean preserving cubes. Never retain block shapes merely to match the source locally.

Priority 3 - Produce photographic materials and lighting:
- Use physically based, real-world materials with fine non-repeating detail at multiple scales.
- Use plausible natural illumination, global illumination, contact shadows, reflections, atmospheric depth, and camera exposure.
- Preserve source color relationships while translating flat game colors into believable material colors.
- The result must look like a photograph captured in a real place, not a game screenshot, voxel render, miniature, toy, diorama, concept painting, or stylized architectural visualization.

Hard exclusions:
- No visible voxel grid, cubes, block seams, one-block steps, pixel art, square foliage clusters, tiled Minecraft textures, low-poly facets, or blocky silhouettes.
- No camera-angle change, reframing, object relocation, major silhouette drift, changed terrain layout, substituted subject, invented object, or decorative addition.
- Do not place text, borders, watermarks, labels, or interface elements in the image.

When exact local detail is ambiguous, choose the simplest physically plausible surface interpretation that preserves the source object's identity and broad silhouette. Add material micro-detail only; do not add new semantic objects.
